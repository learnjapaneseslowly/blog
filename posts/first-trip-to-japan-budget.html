How to Plan Your First Trip to Japan on a Small Budget

Short answer: yes, Japan works on a small budget — if you pick one or two regions and keep the plan simple. Trying to see the whole country in one week is what makes a trip expensive and tiring.

Where should I go on a first trip?

Pick one base city and one day trip, not five cities in seven days.

BaseGood forEasy day trip
TokyoCity life, food, subculture, museumsKamakura or Nikko
KyotoTemples, old streets, gardensNara or Uji

How do I save on places to stay?

  • Business hotels: small, clean, and usually cheaper than tourist hotels.
  • Hostels and capsule hotels: the cheapest beds in big cities.
  • Stay in one place: moving hotels every night costs time and money.
  • Book early: prices rise closer to the date, especially in spring and autumn.

How do I save on food?

Eat where locals eat: convenience stores for breakfast, noodle shops and set-meal restaurants (teishoku) for lunch, and supermarkets after 7 pm, when ready-made meals are often discounted. A good meal in Japan does not need a famous restaurant.

How do I get around cheaply?

Walk as much as you can, and use a prepaid transport card (IC card) for trains and buses. For travel between cities, compare a rail pass with single tickets before you buy — a pass only saves money if you travel a lot. Check the current prices and rules before your trip, as fares and passes change.

What should I prepare before I go?

  • Cash and card: carry some cash — small shops and rural places may not take cards.
  • Internet: a pocket Wi-Fi or a local SIM, so you can use maps and translation.
  • A few Japanese words: greetings, “thank you,” and numbers help a lot. Start with 10 Japanese Words Every Beginner Should Learn First.
  • Luggage: travel light — stations have stairs, and trains get crowded.

How much should I plan per day?

Plan your days loosely: one main thing in the morning, one in the afternoon, and free time in the evening. A trip with empty time in it is a trip you actually enjoy.
