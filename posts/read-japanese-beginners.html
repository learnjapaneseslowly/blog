How to Start Reading Japanese When You Know Only a Few Words

Short answer: read short texts where you already know some of the words, one sentence at a time. Seven minutes a day with one short text — read it three times, learn 1–3 new words from it, and write it in your reading log — is enough to start.

What should I read first?

Pick something short and familiar: a beginner dialogue, one page of a picture book, a simple menu, or a short message you have already heard. The text should be 3–5 sentences long. Long articles and novels can wait — right now, short and repeatable beats long and impressive.

What do I do in those 7 minutes?

Read the same short text three times, with a different job each time.

ReadTimeDo this
1st2 minJust read. Do not look anything up. Notice the words you recognise — even one or two counts.
2nd3 minRead again slowly. Look up 1–3 new words only, and write one example sentence for each.
3rd2 minRead it once more from the top. It should feel easier now — that is the point.

Why read the same text three times?

The first read shows you what you already know. The second read adds a few new words. The third read turns those words into something you can recognise without stopping. A new text every day feels like progress, but the same short text three times teaches you more — and tomorrow you can pick a new one.

What if I can only read a few words?

That is the starting point, not a problem. Count what you recognise, not what you miss. If you spotted 水 (water), 猫 (cat), and 本 (book) in a short text, you read well. Next week, the same kind of text will give you a few more words. Progress in reading is quiet — it shows up as a sentence you suddenly understand without translating word by word.

How do I keep track of it?

Keep a short reading log: date, what you read, how long, and the words you learned. One line a day is enough. Put new words from reading straight into your vocabulary list, with a review date — the schedule in How to Remember Japanese Words Without Forgetting Them works for these too.

Where does reading fit in my routine?

It is the 7-minute input block in How to Learn Japanese in 15 Minutes a Day: 5 minutes of new words, 7 minutes of listening or reading, 3 minutes of review.
