How to Start Minimalism in Japan: Less Stuff, More Calm

Short answer: start with one drawer, not your whole home. Minimalism is not about empty white rooms. It is about keeping what you use and love, and letting the rest go — slowly, one small area at a time.

Why does minimalism fit Japan so well?

Japanese homes are often small, so clutter shows up fast. At the same time, Japan has a long tradition of simple, useful design — from tatami rooms to the idea of mottainai, not wasting what still has value. Minimalism here is less about buying new “minimal” things and more about using what you already have.

Where should I start?

  • One drawer: empty it, keep what you used in the last month, remove the rest.
  • One shelf: books you will actually re-read stay; the rest go.
  • One bag: what do you carry every day? Take out what you never use.
  • One digital folder: delete duplicate photos and unused apps.

What do I do with the things I remove?

In Japan, use the right route for each thing: sell usable items at a second-hand shop or online, donate clothes and books, and recycle or dispose of the rest on the correct collection day for your city. Check your city office's rules — sorting rules differ by area.

How do I stop buying more?

Use a waiting rule: wait 7 days before buying anything that is not food or a replacement. Most wants fade in a week. And keep one rule for what comes in: one thing in, one thing out.

What does minimalism actually give you?

Less cleaning, less searching, and less deciding. A small, calm space is easier to live in — and easier to keep. Start with 15 minutes a day, the same way a small habit works in How to Learn Japanese in 15 Minutes a Day.
