How to Remember Japanese Words Without Forgetting Them

Short answer: you will forget new words — and that is normal. The fix is not studying harder. It is reviewing each word on a short schedule: the next day, three days later, and one week later.

Why do I forget words I just learned?

Memory fades fast at first. A word you meet once is usually gone the next day. A word you meet again the next day lasts longer, and a word you meet a third time a few days later starts to stick. Forgetting is not failure — it is the signal to review.

What is the review schedule?

Learn 5–10 words a day, then review each word three times. That is all the schedule needs to be.

ReviewWhenDo this
1stNext dayCover the meaning. Say the reading and one example sentence from memory.
2nd3 days laterReview again. If you know it, move its review date forward. If not, start the schedule over for that word.
3rd1 week laterOne last check. Words you know three times in a row are the ones you keep.

How do I review without it taking all day?

Keep every word in one list, with a review date next to it. Each day, review only the words whose date is today — usually 5–15 words, about 3 minutes. Do not re-study the whole list. The date tells you what to do, so you never have to decide.

What makes a word easier to remember?

Three small habits help more than extra study time:

  • One example sentence. A word inside a sentence is easier to recall than a word on its own. Write one real sentence for each word.
  • Say it aloud. Reading, meaning, and sound together make a stronger memory than reading alone.
  • Mark honestly. Only mark a word “known” when you recalled it without looking. A list full of words you half-know helps nobody.

How many words should I learn at once?

Five to ten a day is enough. More than that, and the review pile grows faster than you can clear it. Slow and steady is the whole idea — see How to Learn Japanese in 15 Minutes a Day for the daily routine this fits into.
